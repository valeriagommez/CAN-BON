About CAN BON

Nature is undergoing a major transformation due to climate change.

Biodiversity loss is accelerating and with it the many economic and cultural benefits we derive from healthy ecosystems. At the same time, demand for decision-useful data is increasing and there is a great need to mainstream information to support investment decisions.

However, information is fragmented across universities, governments, NGOs, Indigenous organizations, and private organizations, making it difficult to effectively inform policy and meet targets.

What is a BON?

A Biodiversity Observation Network (BON) is a system of observation sites and groups who produce and use biodiversity data for different needs. Other countries (Colombia, France, China, Japan) and regions (Europe, Asia-Pacific, Arctic) have established BONs to support nature policy and environmental legislation.

The Group on Earth Observations Biodiversity Observation Network (GEO BON) Secretariat is currently headquartered in Canada, positioning it well to support the development of CAN BON.

A Canadian BON would...

  • Build a coordinated national system for biodiversity monitoring by connecting observation systems, data infrastructure, and Indigenous knowledge
  • Support modelling and forecasting of biodiversity trends
  • Improve biodiversity monitoring standards and data accessibility
  • Generate decision-useful information for conservation policy and investment
  • Enhance capacity to meet national priorities (30x30, species-at-risk protection, water security, and natural capital accounting,) and international commitments
